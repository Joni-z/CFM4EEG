# Frozen PAC erasure across four EEG models

TUEV shows different patterns across models. CfFT and LaBraM are much more sensitive to erasure at the selected encoder layer than at the tokenizer. TFM and CBraMod are more sensitive at the tokenizer, with CBraMod also showing a substantial encoder drop. On the CfFT and LaBraM encoders, the TFM tokenizer and both CBraMod sites, shuffling the PAC targets before fitting the eraser yields a comparably large drop. These interventions identify subspaces that matter to the frozen classifiers. Attribution to the measured PAC targets depends on their difference from the shuffled-target control.

On the CHB-MIT natural-grid split, CfFT's PR-AUC after PAC erasure is close to the original at both endpoints. Both CBraMod encoder erasers reduce PR-AUC from 0.169 to approximately 0.044, while PAC erasure of its tokenizer changes PR-AUC from 0.169 to 0.183. TFM's tokenizer drops from 0.363 to 0.126 under PAC erasure and to 0.112 under shuffled-target erasure. Its interval for the difference from the shuffled control includes zero. LaBraM's tokenizer shows a larger drop for PAC erasure than for shuffled-target erasure, with a paired recording-resample interval of 0.012–0.056 for that difference. The intervals describe recordings within the two test patients.

We erased train-fitted directions for three PAC target families in two datasets, using frozen task checkpoints. For every intervention site, the comparison includes the original model, one shuffled-target eraser and five rank-matched random erasers. Each eraser subtracts the fitted projection of the pooled window representation from all tokens. Encoder layers are selected by PAC readout on the validation split. TFM's tokenizer endpoint is the native classifier embedding immediately after the code lookup.

TUEV reports Cohen's κ on the accepted shared16 cohort of 29,421 windows. CHB-MIT reports trapezoidal PR-AUC on 17,224 natural-grid windows from two test patients. Each comparison uses identical windows and the same task checkpoint within a model.
