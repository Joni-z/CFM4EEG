\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several cells of Table~\ref{tab:main} are single seeds, including every main-model cell outside TUEV; the three-axis versus two-axis comparison is confounded with parameter count; the learned filterbank has not been compared with fixed log-spaced bands; the pretrained baseline rows were scored under the earlier protocol rather than the native pipeline; TUEV's test set weights repeated signals by annotation row; and CHB-MIT has no completed result on the natural-grid split. The controlled-signal experiments T1, T2 and T6 and the noise ladder of T3 are specified but not yet run, and the CHB-MIT arm of the learned-row control is still training. Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:remaining} lists the remaining runs in order; none changes the direction of the evidence already in hand, and all are bounded.

A clinical EEG label is a simple functional of the demodulated band content of the signal, and a model that computes that content explicitly and reads it along its natural axes needs 1.6M parameters and no pretraining to lead on epileptiform event and seizure corpora. The parts of the model that carried the score were the ones the hypothesis names: band separation, the envelope view for discharges, the waveform view for seizures, sub-second resolution, and factorized attention. The question a tokenizer paper should answer first is of what the label is a function, and the evidence for an answer is a matched control under a fixed reader.
